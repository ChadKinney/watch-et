Your Apple Watch Takes a Beating. Guard It.

November 10, 2026

An Apple Watch is the watch most of us actually wear every day. It tracks the workout, takes the call, pays at the register, and counts the steps. It also rides on the wrist that does the work, so the screen and case take hits from morning to night. Here is how to keep yours looking new.

The gym is the first danger zone

Barbells, dumbbells, racks, and cable machines are all harder than a watch screen. One bump against a plate edge can scratch the glass or dent the case. The watch also collects sweat all workout long, and sweat dries into a film on the screen and sensors. Cover the watch before you lift, and wipe the screen and the sensor on the back when you finish.

Work puts it through more

Tools, ladders, tailgates, concrete, and door frames. If your job uses your hands, your watch is in the line of fire all day. The screen faces out, so it takes the first hit every time your wrist brushes something. A sleeve over the watch takes those hits instead, and the flap keeps the screen covered until you need to see it.

Outdoors, it earns its keep and takes its lumps

Trail runs, hikes, rucks, yard work, and weekends on the water all put the watch against rocks, branches, pack straps, and sand. Sunscreen and bug spray smear the screen, and grit in a pocket or a pack scratches it when you set the watch down. Keep it covered while you move, and give the screen a wipe with a soft cloth when you get back.

Night and day, it stays on

Plenty of folks sleep with the watch on to track sleep, then wear it straight through the next day. That means it rarely gets a break. Give it a quick clean once a day, check the band for wear, and let the sensors breathe. A clean sensor reads better, and a clean screen is easier to read in the sun.
